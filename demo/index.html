The order they go in

These are not five unrelated calculators. They are one argument, and each tool answers the question the previous one raises.

Does it fit? → the KV cache is what runs out → what does KV cost? → it depends on how the pool is shared → how does the scheduler share it? → a full batch leaves compute idle → can we spend that? → every one of these interacts → the console.

Why you can trust the numbers

Every page carries a copy of the model, which is exactly the kind of thing that rots. So none of it is taken on trust: CI re-derives it on every push.

—configurations checked against the notebook's Python, exact on decisions and 1e-9 on numbers
every pageGPU and model catalogs compared field by field, so a table edited in one place cannot disagree with another
both wayseach check is verified to fail when the model is wrong — a checker that cannot fail proves nothing

These pages are planning models, not benchmarks. They assume achievable fractions of peak bandwidth and FLOPs, and a typical engine's per-step overhead. Use them to shortlist configurations and to understand which term binds; then measure the shortlist on the real hardware, open-loop, and fold your own numbers back in.

Built from the serving track of gpu-training-notebooks — 23 notebooks that derive all of this from first principles and measure it on real engines, 18 of which need no GPU at all.
