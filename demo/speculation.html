Speculative decoding, and when it backfires

A small draft model guesses the next few tokens; the big model checks them all in one pass. Every accepted guess is a token you got for the price of a verification — and the output is identical to what the big model would have produced alone. The catch is that rejected guesses are not free, and at high batch sizes there is no spare compute to gamble with.

Speedup against draft length, for a range of acceptance rates

Longer drafts help only while the acceptance rate holds up. The curves turn over because the chance of getting all k right decays geometrically while the cost of drafting them does not.

Speculation is lossless: the verification step guarantees the accepted tokens are exactly the ones the target model would have sampled. What you are trading is compute for latency, which is why it only pays when you have compute to spare.
